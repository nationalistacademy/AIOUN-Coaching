# Client Enquiry, Engagement and Delivery Workflow

## Purpose

Provide a consistent and respectful process for professional coaching and consultancy-style engagements. This workflow is a proposed operating procedure; activate it only after the proprietor approves availability, pricing and policies.

## 1. Enquiry received

Capture only what is needed:
- Name or preferred form of address.
- Reply email or chosen contact method.
- Broad service category.
- Short description of the requested outcome.
- Preferred time window, if a session is requested.

Do not request CNIC, passwords, account access, student portal credentials, bank details, health information or full legal case files in the initial form.

Send an acknowledgement that explains expected response time only if the proprietor has approved that service level.

## 2. Suitability and risk check

Before quoting, ask internally:
- Is the request within the published service scope?
- Does it require a regulated professional or licensed advocate?
- Is there a conflict of interest or confidentiality concern?
- Is the deadline realistic?
- Is the client asking for cheating, impersonation, forged documents, fabricated research or unlawful activity?
- Can the operator competently deliver the work?
- Is sensitive data genuinely necessary?

Decline or refer requests that are outside competence, unsafe, unlawful or regulated. Do not make an urgent legal deadline assessment; advise the person to contact a qualified lawyer or relevant authority promptly.

## 3. Written proposal and acceptance

Send a written proposal containing:
- Service description and exclusions.
- Deliverables.
- Client responsibilities.
- Fee and payment stages.
- Estimated timeline.
- Revision allowance.
- Privacy and data handling summary.
- Cancellation/refund terms.
- Relevant disclaimer.

Begin only after the client explicitly accepts the proposal and any agreed payment condition is met.

## 4. Work and quality assurance

- Keep work within the agreed scope.
- Use reliable and current sources for factual claims.
- Keep client materials private and access-limited.
- Do not upload confidential material to AI or third-party tools without informed consent and a legitimate need.
- Review generated content for accuracy, originality, privacy and suitability.
- Keep a record of material scope changes and obtain agreement before extra work.

## 5. Draft review and revisions

Provide a draft where the engagement includes one. Identify what the client should verify. Apply only the included revision rounds; quote new work separately before proceeding.

For coaching, provide session notes or an action plan only where included in the agreed scope.

## 6. Final delivery and closure

- Deliver through the agreed channel.
- Confirm the files open and are in the agreed format.
- Remind the client of their responsibilities and any independent verification needed.
- Confirm closure and invite factual feedback without pressure.
- Do not publish testimonials, names, screenshots or work samples without specific permission.

## 7. Records and retention

Maintain the minimum record needed for service delivery, accounting and dispute handling. Restrict access. Set a retention period in the final privacy policy, then securely delete or anonymize records when no longer required, subject to applicable legal recordkeeping obligations.

Do not keep passwords, OTPs, unnecessary identity documents or payment-card details.

## 8. Complaints and corrections

Acknowledge complaints through the published official contact channel. Record the issue, investigate, explain the outcome and offer a fair correction or remedy where appropriate. Do not retaliate against clients or suppress genuine criticism.

## 9. Human approval

The proprietor must approve:
- Every quotation and paid engagement.
- Any referral or regulated-service decision.
- Final client deliverables.
- Public testimonials and case studies.
- Any external email or message sent on behalf of the business.

AI agents may prepare drafts and checklists but must not independently accept clients, set unapproved prices, make legal determinations, or send messages.
